PVD color QC: why the same color shifts under different lighting

Color consistency checklist

Standardize these items to reduce lighting-driven complaints and batch drift.

  • Define a reference light (e.g., D65) and viewing angle.
  • Keep substrate prep (polish / base layer) consistent.
  • Document an approved reference sample and acceptance tolerance.

What is metamerism and why it matters

A surface can look different under warm versus cool lighting. This is metamerism, and it is more visible on metallic, luxury finishes.

For premium projects, controlling display and installation lighting is part of QC.

What actually shifts the perceived color

Surface prep, layer stack, fixtures, and even part orientation can change the final perceived hue.

If polishing varies between batches, perceived color will drift even with constant parameters.

A practical consistency workflow

Create an approved reference sample and compare every batch under the same light.

Define tolerance: is “slight angle-dependent shift” acceptable or not?

Frequently asked questions

Can instruments measure the color precisely?

Color meters can help, but glossy/metallic finishes often still require visual comparison under standard light against a reference sample.

Why are large parts harder to keep consistent?

Because coating distribution and surface uniformity are more sensitive, and fixture or polishing differences become more obvious.
