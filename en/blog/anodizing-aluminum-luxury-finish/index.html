What is anodizing?

It forms a controlled oxide layer on aluminum, making the surface harder and more resistant.

The layer can be dyed and can produce appealing matte/satin finishes.

Compared to PVD in luxury projects

PVD is the go-to for special metallic luxury colors (gold/rose/smoke).

Anodizing delivers a durable industrial-lux feel for aluminum, especially matte finishes.

Prep and visual quality

Base scratches directly affect the result; controlled polishing or blasting matters.

For sensitive colors, uniformity control and keeping a master sample is recommended.

Recommended applications

Aluminum hardware, architectural profiles, and luxury parts with matte/satin finish.

For production, standardizing prep improves color and process consistency.

FAQ

FAQ

Is anodizing for steel too?

Anodizing is primarily for aluminum; steel uses other routes like paint/powder/vacuum coatings.

Is it suitable for outdoor use?

Often yes, but the anodizing type and quality control matter.

What are anodizing limits on color/gloss?

Color and gloss depend on alloy, preparation, and process; some alloys are more prone to color shift or blotching. For luxury results, tight control and sampling are essential.

Can anodizing be combined with PVD?

In some scenarios it can work, but layer compatibility and adhesion must be validated—typically via samples and adhesion tests.

Is sampling necessary?

For luxury or volume projects, sampling reduces risk of color variation and defects.

Any key care tip?

Avoid abrasives and very strong cleaners; use a soft cloth.
