Quick takeaways

If you plan volume production or a luxury finish, we recommend sampling before final production.

  • Reliable ABS route: cleaning + compatible primer + base layer + PVD + final protection.
  • Outgassing and moisture are common causes of haze/peeling; thorough drying is critical.
  • For high-touch parts, a proper clear topcoat determines durability and fingerprint resistance.
  • In production, standardize prep and fixturing to reduce color variation.

Why direct PVD on ABS is challenging

ABS is heat-sensitive and can deform in vacuum/plasma, so the process must be low-temperature and controlled.

ABS has low surface energy; without activation/primer, peeling and scratch risk increases.

Common coating routes for ABS

Route 1 (luxury decorative): basecoat/primer → PVD (e.g., sputtering) → clear hard topcoat.

Route 2 (vacuum metallizing): basecoat → metal evaporation → protective lacquer; cost-effective metallic look.

For abrasion-prone parts, a suitable topcoat (hard clear/UV) strongly affects durability.

Surface preparation (the key to adhesion)

Thorough cleaning/degreasing, then plasma/corona activation or ABS-specific primers.

Controlling primer thickness and cure time improves color uniformity and reduces orange-peel.

Recommended quality checks

Adhesion (cross-cut), visual uniformity, and wear/spot checks are recommended for high-traffic use.

For batch production, keep an approved sample as the color/finish master.

FAQ

FAQ

Is PVD on ABS scratch-proof?

PVD is thin; final durability depends on primer/topcoat. A hard topcoat is essential for high-traffic parts.

What matters for large ABS parts?

Temperature control and uniform basecoat thickness are critical to avoid warping and color variation.

Can ABS be PVD-coated without a base layer?

A base/interlayer is usually recommended for better uniformity and adhesion. The exact stack is defined during sampling.

What about high-touch ABS parts?

A more durable clear topcoat is often recommended to reduce fingerprints and maintain appearance.

What pretreatments are common for plastics?

For ABS/PP, surface activation (plasma/corona), primer/basecoat, and outgassing control are common.

Is PP harder than ABS for coating?

Usually yes—PP has lower surface energy, requiring stronger activation and suitable primers.

Recommended step-by-step route for PVD on ABS

ABS typically needs a stable underlayer (primer/interlayer) before PVD to improve adhesion and reduce peeling risk.

Final quality depends on: surface cleanliness, interlayer compatibility, and controlling humidity/outgassing in vacuum.

  1. Verify material: true ABS (not blends) + check mold release/oils.
  2. Industrial cleaning/degreasing, then complete drying (no moisture).
  3. Controlled micro-roughening and a suitable primer (with adhesion testing).
  4. Add a conductive/metallized base if required, then color PVD / multilayer stack.
  5. Apply a clear topcoat/protection for scratch/UV resistance (especially decorative parts).
