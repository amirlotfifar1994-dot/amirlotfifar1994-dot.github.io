A transparent process from brief to delivery

Every stage should have a clear input, decision and output

The exact route changes by service, but the project logic stays consistent: define the problem, clarify acceptance criteria, then prepare, execute and review quality.

Project execution map

Six stages with understandable outputs

Instead of only naming operations, each stage shows what information is needed and what decision comes next.

  1. 01

    Brief and initial review

    Material, application, quantity, dimensions, surface condition, target finish and deadline are collected.

    Input
    Photos and project details
    Output
    Review route and next technical questions
  2. 02

    Reference and acceptance criteria

    For sensitive colors or projects, finish references and expected limits are clarified before execution.

    Input
    Sample/reference and use conditions
    Output
    Defined finish and acceptance points
  3. 03

    Surface preparation

    Cleaning, polishing, preparation or removal of surface marks is defined by the part condition.

    Input
    Part and existing surface
    Output
    Surface ready for the next process
  4. 04

    Coating process execution

    The suitable process is performed according to the selected service and project parameters.

    Input
    Approved surface and technical route
    Output
    Coated part ready for review
  5. 05

    Output review

    Finish, consistency and agreed items are reviewed before delivery.

    Input
    Final part and acceptance reference
    Output
    Approval for packing or further review
  6. 06

    Surface protection and delivery

    The final surface is separated and prepared for transport according to its sensitivity.

    Input
    Approved part
    Output
    Delivery-ready part with required care guidance
Before sending the part

Four details that reduce back-and-forth questions

A more complete brief means a technical route and schedule can be proposed with less ambiguity.

Material and surface conditionMention raw, polished, plated, painted or previously coated surfaces.
Real applicationDescribe environment, touch, moisture, cleaners, abrasion or working temperature.
Quantity and dimensionsInclude approximate batch size and sample dimensions or weight.
Finish and deadlineSpecify the target color/finish reference and expected delivery timing.
Repeat project

Start with a stable sample and criterion

If orders will repeat across batches, defining a reference and documenting visual expectations before production becomes more important.

Used part

Start with surface condition and restoration feasibility

Corrosion, scratches or previous coatings can change preparation needs and the achievable result.

Color-sensitive

Start with a physical reference or sampling

When small color differences matter, making a decision from photos alone carries more risk.

Decision gates

A project should move forward only when the previous question has a sufficient answer

A good process is not about adding steps; it is about removing ambiguous decisions. When information is insufficient, returning to clarification or sampling is safer than carrying uncertainty into production.

01

Is the part problem clear?

Substrate, current surface condition, application and visual target should be clear enough to eliminate unsuitable routes.

02

Is the reference sufficient?

For color- or appearance-sensitive work, a photo may only be the starting point. A physical reference or sample may be needed to define expectations more precisely.

03

Is delivery acceptance understandable?

Before execution, the basis for reviewing the output should be clear, together with substrate or application limitations that affect the decision.

If one of these gates does not have a sufficient answer, “more information” is not unnecessary delay; it is part of project risk control.
